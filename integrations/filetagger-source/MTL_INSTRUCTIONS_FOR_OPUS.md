## THE THREE LAYERS

Every equation needs THREE things, not just one:

### LAYER 1: The Equation (Column C — already filled)
This is the raw LaTeX. Leave it alone.

Example:
```
χ = ∭(G·M·E·S·T·K·R·Q·F·C) dx dy dt
```

### LAYER 2: The Word-Form Equation (Column F — "English_Translation")
THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT COLUMN.

Replace every symbol with the word or short phrase that best represents
what that symbol MEANS — but KEEP THE MATHEMATICAL STRUCTURE.

The reader should see the SAME equation shape but with words instead of symbols.

Example for χ = ∭(G·M·E·S·T·K·R·Q·F·C) dx dy dt:
```
Total Coherence = ∭(Grace × Moral Force × Truth × Entropy × Time ×
Knowledge × Relational Frame × Quantum State × Faith × Christ Alignment)
across all space and time
```

Example for dS/dt ≥ 0:
```
Change in Disorder / Change in Time ≥ 0
(Disorder always increases or stays the same — never spontaneously reverses)
```

Example for F = -∇V(r):
```
Force on the soul = negative gradient of the Spiritual Potential at distance r
(The pull toward sin increases as distance from God increases)
```

RULES FOR THIS COLUMN:
- Keep the mathematical operators (+, -, ×, /, =, ≥, ∫, ∂, Σ)
- Replace EVERY variable with its best plain English word
- If a variable could mean different things in different contexts,
  use the meaning that fits THIS article (check the Context column)
- Use parentheses and line breaks to keep it readable
- After the word-form equation, add one sentence in parentheses
  explaining what the whole thing SAYS

### LAYER 3: Term-by-Term Breakdown (Column G — "Term_By_Term")
List each variable/symbol and what it means. Format:

```
χ = total coherence (0 = complete collapse, 1 = perfect alignment)
G = grace intensity (external negentropy input)
M = moral force (accumulated moral weight)
E = truth signal (information fidelity)
S = entropy (accumulated disorder)
T = time coordinate
∭ = integration across all space and time
```

For large equations, focus on the 4-5 MOST IMPORTANT structural features.
Not every subscript needs a line — just the load-bearing variables.
